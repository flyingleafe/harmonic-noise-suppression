## The frozen definition

The temperature is calibrated in **one direction only**: a **scalar log-power gain** $\eta$
applied to the model mean, $M_i\mapsto M_i e^{\eta}$, evaluated on the **untempered actual fit
risk** (not the per-second-normalized reporting form).

For a baseline predictive draw $b$ with periodogram $I_b$:

$$
U_b=\sum_i a_i\left(1-\frac{I_{b,i}}{M_i}\right),
\qquad
H=\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
J=\operatorname{Var}_b\!\left(U_b\right),
\qquad
\boxed{\,T=J/H\,}
$$

where $\operatorname{Var}_b$ is the **sample** variance over the frozen baseline predictive draws.
The fitted objective is then

$$
\frac{\mathcal L}{T}\;+\;\text{one OU prior} .
$$

$U_b$ is the gradient of the composite risk in the gain direction and $H$ its expected curvature
there, so $T=J/H$ is an ordinary **sandwich** correction: it rescales the objective so that the
curvature-to-variance ratio is right **along that one direction**.

## The constraints that must travel with it

State all of these; they are the part that stops the quantity being misused.

- **Same weights as the fit.** $U_b$, $H$ and $J$ use the **same analysis exposure weights $a_i$
  and the same microphone/bin weights as the fit objective** — **not** the per-unique-second
  normalized $\mathcal L$ used for reporting. The reporting normalization and the fit temperature
  are different quantities and must not be conflated. (The documents already warn that exposure
  bookkeeping and fit temperature are different knobs — tie this to that warning.)
- **Scope: one direction only.** This is a **scalar gain-direction sandwich calibration**. It is
  **not** all-parameter posterior coverage, it does **not** make the composite a likelihood, and it
  supplies **no** posterior curvature or uncertainty for any other parameter. Say so explicitly,
  next to the formula.
- **Determinism and preregistration.** One **fixed master seed**; the **Monte Carlo draw count and
  the supports are preregistered** and frozen in the manifest before any candidate runs.
- **Failure rule.** A **non-positive or non-finite $J$ is a calibration failure** — the instrument
  must fail loudly, not fall back to a default or to $T=1$. This matches the existing rule that a
  manifest lacking its calibration fails loudly rather than defaulting to something permissive.
- **Frozen before candidates.** $T$ is calibrated on **frozen baseline predictive draws** and
  frozen before any candidate is fitted; it is never retuned after seeing candidate results.
- **No values yet.** **No measured $T$ has been computed.** Do not write a number, a range, an
  order of magnitude, or an expectation for $T$. The candidate owner is aligning the
  temperature-divide code; that work is not yours and is not finished.

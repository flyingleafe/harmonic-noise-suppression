# Is the speed error above the label band white or correlated?

Generated by `scripts/noise_v2_residual_acf.py` in 9.1 s on the laptop. No cluster job was needed.

## The question

A rotor-speed label carried at 31.25 Hz holds nothing above 15.625 Hz. The renderer must GENERATE the rest. This study asks, with the simplest statistic available, whether that missing part is white noise or a correlated (red) process.

The residual is `nu_res = 2 pi x (rps high-passed at 16 Hz)` in rad/s, from a 4th-order Butterworth applied with zero phase, on airborne segments of at least 5 s at each rig's own logging rate. A high-pass makes even white noise look correlated, so every measured autocorrelation is shown against a NULL: white noise of the same length through the same filter, averaged over 8 draws.

Verdict rule, fixed before the data was read: RED when the PSD slope on 16 Hz – 0.45 fs is at most -1 AND the measured autocorrelation stands above the filtered-white null at the measured 1/e lag by more than the white band `2/sqrt(N)`. WHITE otherwise.

## What this means

7 of 8 rigs read RED (neurobem_quad, blackbird_quad, vid_m100, nanobench_cf21b, pitcn_quad, dregon_room1, dregon_room1_command) and 0 read WHITE (none). A RED rig needs a correlated generator above the label band; a WHITE rig is served by plain white speed noise of the measured rms.

Every measured curve swings below zero, down to -0.91 at the worst rig against -0.44 for the deepest null, and crosses zero between 8.6 ms and 14.3 ms. That is the signature of power in a narrow band just above the corner, not of white noise: the residual rings at the edge of the label band and then dies.

Every number above is in `residual.json` in this directory, per rotor and pooled.
